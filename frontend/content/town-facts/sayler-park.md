# Sayler Park (Cincinnati, OH 45233): verified local facts

## Fix in the existing site copy first
- `locations.ts` says Sayler Park was annexed "in the early 1900s". Sources give 1911. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayler_Park,_Cincinnati
- `locations.ts` says it sits "past Riverside" and lists Riverside and Sedamsville as adjacent. Order east to west is Sedamsville, Riverside, Sayler Park, so Riverside is the neighbour and Sedamsville is not. https://uacvoice.org/2022/06/cincinnatis-western-riverfront-neighborhoods-sedamsville-riverside-sayler-park-a-new-study-by-core-member-michael-maloney-by-mike-templeton/
- "Sits in the Ohio River floodplain", "galvanized supply lines" and "sewer backups after high water" are unverified. No source found for Sayler Park flood history, FEMA zone status or pipe materials. Don't use as fact.
- ZIP 45233 is wider than the neighbourhood: its ACS population is 15,981 against 2,825 for Sayler Park, so ZIP-level housing data is not a proxy. https://censusreporter.org/profiles/86000US45233-45233/

## Where it is
- Sayler Park is a Cincinnati neighbourhood in Hamilton County, the westernmost in the city, on the Ohio River. Originally called Home City; annexed by Cincinnati in 1911. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayler_Park,_Cincinnati
- 2020 Census: 2,825 residents (the city's Statistical Neighborhood Approximation uses Census Tracts 105 and 106). https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/sites/planning/assets/2020%20CENSUS/SaylerPark_2020.pdf
- A National Park Service document describes it as a predominantly middle-class residential community, built up in the late 19th to mid-20th centuries, "confined by steep hills to the north and heavily traveled U.S. Route 50 to the south". https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/010626ca-0784-42d7-8b7b-f2aa377cf723 (snippet only; document not opened, so treat as partly verified)
- The 1974 Super Outbreak F5 tornado struck the neighbourhood and destroyed several homes. https://nkytribune.com/2025/04/the-river-the-1974-saylor-park-tornado-remembering-its-51st-anniversary
- Short Woods Park Mound is on the National Register (1974). No National Register or local historic district covering Sayler Park homes was found: unverified. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Woods_Park_Mound

## Housing stock (ACS 2020-2024 5-year, Census Tracts 105 and 106, Hamilton County, combined by me)
Sources: https://censusreporter.org/profiles/14000US39061010500-census-tract-105-hamilton-oh/ and https://censusreporter.org/profiles/14000US39061010600-census-tract-106-hamilton-oh/
- Median year built: 1946 (tract 105) and 1938 (tract 106). No combined median.
- Of 1,256 housing units, 638 (50.8%) were built in 1939 or earlier, 690 (54.9%) in 1949 or earlier. Only 83 (6.6%) date from 2000 or later.
- Units in structure: 791 single-family detached, 35 attached, 152 two-family, 95 in 3-4 unit buildings, 82 in 5-9, 54 in 10-19, 47 mobile homes.
- Tenure: 720 of 1,183 occupied units owner-occupied (60.9%), 463 rented (39.1%).
- Heating fuel: 737 utility gas (62.3%), 420 electricity (35.5%), 17 propane, 2 fuel oil, 7 none.
- Tract 105 is older, denser and more rental (292 of 594 renters) than tract 106 (171 of 589).

## Permits
- Sayler Park is inside the City of Cincinnati, so the Department of Buildings & Inspections issues building and mechanical permits (confirmed for AC installation or replacement in the Sedamsville facts file). https://cincinnati-oh.gov/buildings/building-construction-inspections/mechanical-inspections
- Plumbing and electrical permit issuer, fees and timings: unverified (the city site was unreachable during this research).

## Utility
- Electric utility: Duke Energy Ohio (all of the City of Cincinnati). Rebate amounts unverified.

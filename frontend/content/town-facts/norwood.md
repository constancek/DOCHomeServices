# Norwood, OH: verified facts for the AC repair page (slug: norwood)

Existing site copy (locations.ts, ac-repair-parts/part1.ts) says: ZIP 45212, its own city entirely surrounded by Cincinnati, dense early-1900s homes and brick two-families, streetcar and factory town, Surrey Square, Montgomery Road. The Census data below backs up the "century-old" and "two-families" claims.

## 1. Where it is
- Independent city in Hamilton County. Village incorporated May 14, 1888 (earlier called Sharpsburg). Became a city by Mayor George E. Mills' proclamation on May 4, 1903. https://www.norwoodohiohistoricalsociety.org/BriefHistory.html , https://www.norwoodohiohistoricalsociety.org/date1900s.html
- Population 19,114 (ACS 2024 5-yr). 2020 Census count: 19,043. https://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US3957386-norwood-oh/
- About 3.14 sq mi, entirely surrounded by Cincinnati. Bordering areas include Evanston, Avondale and Hyde Park (Wikipedia, which doesn't give a full list. Oakley and Pleasant Ridge also border it per the site's adjacency list, but I didn't verify that).
- Roads: Montgomery Road (SR 3), Smith Rd/Carthage Ave, the Norwood Lateral (SR 562, finished into Norwood in 1977) and I-71 on the east side. Building the Lateral razed nearly 200 East Norwood homes (Wikipedia, needs a primary source).
- Landmarks: Surrey Square (1970s, redone in 2007), Rookwood Pavilion/Commons/Exchange, and the site of the GM Norwood Assembly plant (1923 to 1987). Norwood Mound (Adena, on the NRHP) sits in Water Tower Park, the city's highest point. https://norwoodohiohistoricalsociety.org/date2000s.html
- Railroad suburb: by 1894, "nearly seventy passenger trains a day" stopped at six Norwood stations. https://www.norwoodohiohistoricalsociety.org/BriefHistory.html

## 2. Housing stock (ACS 2024 5-year, tables B25035/B25034/B25024/B25003)
Source: https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B25035,B25034,B25024,B25003&geo_ids=16000US3957386
- **Median year built: 1938.** Hamilton County: 1963 (ACS 2024 1-yr).
- **6,701 of 10,084 units (66.5%) built 1939 or earlier.** Countywide figure: 25.4% (97,255 of 383,004).
- 1940-59: 1,319 units (13.1%). 1960-79: 1,188 (11.8%). Built 1980 or later: only 876 (8.7%). Just 17 units built in 2000-09.
- Units in structure: 5,400 single detached (53.6%), 1,169 two-unit (11.6%), 1,502 three-to-four-unit (14.9%). So 26.5% of homes are in 2-4 unit buildings. Another 1,895 units are in buildings with 5 or more units.
- Tenure: 4,672 of 8,899 occupied units are rented (52.5%), 4,227 owner-occupied.
- Median owner-occupied home value: $258,700.

## 3. What that means for AC (HVAC judgment tied to the data, no outside source)
- Two-thirds of homes went up before 1940, before residential central air, so any central AC is a retrofit. Expect ducts run through attics or closets, original wiring and panels that may need an upgrade for a new condenser circuit, and window units or ductless mini-splits where ducts won't fit.
- 26.5% of units are in 2-4 unit buildings and 52.5% of households rent. That means separate systems per unit, side-by-side condensers on small lots, and landlord/tenant decisions about repair versus replacement.
- Almost no 1990s-2000s subdivision stock exists here (17 units built 2000-09), so the "original builder-grade unit hitting replacement age" angle doesn't fit Norwood.

## 4. Permits
- The **City of Norwood Building Department** issues the HVAC permit. Its HVAC Permit Application covers "ductwork, furnaces, and air conditioning" and needs a site plan. Phone 513-458-4510, nbd@norwoodohio.gov. Applications can't be emailed. Fees went up 1/2/2026. Plumbing permits go through Hamilton County Public Health instead. https://www.norwoodohio.gov/162/building-department
- The state licenses HVAC contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board (OCILB). That's general Ohio knowledge and I didn't fetch a source for it.

## 5. Utility and rebates
- Duke Energy Ohio delivers the power. Only directory sites confirm this (https://electricrates.org/ohio/norwood/). I couldn't verify a city electric aggregation program.
- **No current Duke Energy Ohio residential HVAC rebate.** House Bill 6 ended Ohio utility efficiency programs. PUCO set rebate applications to stop Sept 30, 2020 and the programs to wind down by Dec 31, 2020, and it rejected Duke's voluntary plan. https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/utilities/duke-energy-ohio-efficiency-plan , https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/?p=22699 . HB 79 would let utilities bring programs back, but its status is unverified. Don't promise rebates.

## 6. Climate (NWS Wilmington, CVG airport station, which is in Kentucky)
- July normal high is 86°F and the July normal low is about 66°F (NWS CVG July normals sheet, made in 2021, so probably 1991-2020 normals). https://www.weather.gov/media/iln/climo_webpage/CVG_Jul.pdf
- About 21 days a year reach 90°F or higher in Cincinnati (1981-2010 normals). The 1991-2020 count is unverified. https://www.weather.gov/iln/90s
- July 2020 had 16 days at 90°F or higher at CVG, the most in a month since 2012. https://www.weather.gov/media/iln/climate_summary/ClimateReport_July2020.pdf
- Norwood-specific heat island or humidity data: none found. Leave it out.

## 7. Other local notes
- I didn't find a Norwood historic-district rule on condenser placement. The NRHP listings found are single sites (Municipal Building, Norwood Mound), not districts. I also didn't find a floodplain issue. Norwood sits on upland about 660 ft up, not river flat (elevation from Wikipedia).
- The city also enforces the Norwood Housing and Property Code on residential properties (same building dept URL).
